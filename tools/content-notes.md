Editorial checks, 2026-10-30:

- Used to describes past habits and states with a past/present contrast. Use the standard learner spelling did/didn't use to. Present habits normally use the present simple.
- Habitual would describes repeated past events with a past context. It is not excluded from habits, despite one misleading sentence in the photographed table. It does not independently establish whether the habit still continues.
- Do not give a universal ban on would be: the restriction taught here concerns ongoing states in this habitual-past reading. Other meanings of would and repeated situations exist.
- Wouldn't often signals unwillingness/refusal and is not a mechanical negative replacement for didn't use to.
- Be used to describes familiarity; get used to describes becoming familiar. To is a preposition, followed by a noun, pronoun or -ing form. Be/get carry tense. Familiarity need not mean liking something.
- A single dated event takes the past simple, not a past-habit construction.
- Choose tasks explicitly accept multiple correct forms when the stated context supports both. An answer is a model, not the only possible translation. Repair tasks state the intended meaning.
- Learning examples involving work are fictional language practice, not medical recommendations. No clinical efficacy claims are included.
